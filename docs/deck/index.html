Zetaris: hospitals read in place, FDA's record as tables

  • Federated, not copied. Two hospital Postgres servers over JDBC through a read-only account that sees only published views; one hospital's nightly CSV cabinet read as file tables where it lands.
  • FDA as file tables. 40,361 inspections, 46,893 Form 483 observations, 2,972 compliance actions, 1,007 import-alert listings, 12,341 recalls, plant lists from 1,532 label filings.
  • One semantic layer (product, unit_of_use, site, site_fei, product_site, inspection, a stock table per hospital). Lineage shows a stock query as "4 systems, 1 statement".
  • Units of use everywhere. East counts packages, so its activation multiplies by East's own pack size: a transfer is in real vials, per presentation, never pooled.
  • EXPLAIN shows the NDC list pushed to Postgres as In(ndc11, [...]), and a USL table as one opaque scan, so its filters are not pushed down. We report both.

UpstreamMeterless07 / 14

NVIDIA Nemotron writes; it never decides a fact

  • Nemotron 3 Ultra writes the explanation and the P&T memo. Nemotron 3.5 Lightning writes the overnight brief.
  • The state (confirmed, check, no signal) is set by rule, and every sentence must cite evidence ids.
  • A deterministic verifier checks dates, numbers, counts, stock fields, the event and direction of a day gap, plant and classification pairs, causes, status words and absence claims. A sentence that fails is dropped.
  • The memo and brief carry no citations, so they get a grounding check: over 214 recorded memos, 3,380 sentences checked, 38 dropped.
44 of 44

false sentences written by three rounds of judges to slip past it were dropped; every true one tested was kept.

Some of them, as the verifier saw them

  • The OAI letter came 23 days after FDA posted the shortage.
    the evidence says 23 days before, not after
  • The carboplatin shortage stems from the Intas OAI inspection 1191818.
    causal claim ("stems from") the cited record does not make
  • No FDA action at Intas preceded the shortage posting.
    false: the record holds an OAI inspection on 2022-12-02 at Intas
  • Corvale Lakeside has nine days of stock.
    "nine" is a number in words; numbers must be digits from the evidence

It is pattern-based: a strong filter, not a proof. The test sentences are in data/eval/verifier.json.

The back-test: it explains, it does not forecast

Shortages FDA posted from 2022 with a placeable plant (92). Had FDA acted against a plant on the label in the two years before?

Dated when public
13%
Dated when it closed
23%
  • Strictly dated: no lift over products that never went short (1.1×, p 0.85).
  • Close-dated: 1.93× the controls (p 0.012), but that uses hindsight about which inspections became OAI.
  • Counter-example: Baxter, Marion NC. The OAI was real; Hurricane Helene caused the 2024 IV fluid shortage.

A plant's FDA record explains a shortage after the fact; it does not predict one. Upstream is built to explain, cite and act on what is known.

Where the trail exists, FDA acted about three months before the posting (median 98 to 103 days).

Upstream informs; a pharmacist decides

  • A contract per question. Scout signs an execution contract; every tool call is checked against it before it runs. The carboplatin register: 11 entries, 11 allowed, 0 refused.
  • Refusals by policy. Orders, inventory changes, formulary changes and dosing advice are refused with a reason.
  • Named approval. Memory writes are refused until a pharmacist or the director approves. Name and role come from a session the server signs, never from the form; a buyer can draft, and Scout refuses the approval.
  • Single-use, short-lived. The memo's contract is bound to the case and drug and expires after five minutes. The memo itself goes through the verifier.
  • MCP server. Six read-only tools at /api/mcp; each checks its contract for every capability it reads.

What it does not do yet, and what comes next

Limits

  • Corvale Health is fictional. Its stock is synthetic; every NDC, labeler and plant is real FDA data.
  • Labels are incomplete. 23% name no plant and 33% list none in their establishment block; plants that make the active ingredient are almost invisible.
  • Look-ahead bias. Plant lists and import-alert lists are today's.
  • Explains, does not predict. The back-test shows no lift on strictly dated data.
  • The hosted site replays recorded live runs, and its approver sign-in is a demo.

Next

  • Real ERP and inventory connectors through Zetaris. The hospitals are already read in place, so a real system is a new data source, not a copy.
  • Per-tenant semantic-layer views, so each health system sees only its own stock.
  • Single sign-on in place of the demo PINs, feeding the same server-side approval rule.
  • More of FDA's record: drug master files and API plant registrations, to reach the ingredient makers.
  • Alerts from the overnight watch when FDA acts at a plant on our labels.
